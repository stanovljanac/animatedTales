DESCRIPTION:
El Dorado was not a city. It was a man covered in gold dust, standing on a raft in the middle of a mountain lake, and the Spaniards who came hunting for him never saw it.

This is the legend of El Dorado as the chronicler Juan Rodríguez Freyle wrote it down in 1636. An heir of the Muisca spent six years fasting in a cave, was dusted with powdered gold, and from a reed raft in Lake Guatavita, Colombia, threw gold into the water while the shore roared. The Spaniards heard of it in Quito. Then the name left the man and became a place, a city and a lake called Parime on European maps for two hundred years. Walter Raleigh sailed after it twice.

A gold raft found in a cave at Pasca in 1969 may or may not show that lake, and whether the ceremony happened as Freyle tells it, nobody can prove. Freyle himself went hunting for gold at another sacred lake, and he confesses it.
