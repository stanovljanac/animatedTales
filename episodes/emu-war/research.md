# Research — The War Against Birds (stage 03)

TOPIC
Idea #10 in `docs/ideas.md` (scored 81.6, was `scored`): the Emu War of Western Australia, 2 November to 10
December 1932. First Australian story on the channel (fills the Australia region). Popularity is **not
measured** (no view counts from the search tool); the topic is widely known as an internet curiosity.
**Sourcing is weaker than the other episodes**: no primary document could be opened (Trove returns an access
check, Hansard and the National Archives were not reachable by the tools). Everything below is the
Wikipedia article *Emu War* and three local or popular pages. That is stated here and in `docs/ideas.md`.

RECOMMENDED STORY
Soldier-settlers on marginal wheat land, the Depression, falling wheat prices and unkept promises of
subsidies; "as many as 20,000" emus; a deputation to the Minister of Defence, Sir George Pearce, who agrees on
conditions (soldiers work the guns; the state pays transport; farmers pay for ammunition and food). Major
Meredith with two soldiers, two Lewis guns, 10,000 rounds, after rain delayed it; 2 November at Campion; the
birds out of range, the herding attempt, the scatter; the dam ambush of 4 November, over 1,000 emus, a jammed
gun after twelve kills; the truck-mounted gun; 2,500 rounds by 8 November; kills estimated 50 to 500;
"no casualties except for their dignity"; a mocking medal question in Parliament; Pearce withdraws on 8
November; return on 13 November at the Premier's urging; about 40 emus in two days; about 100 a week by
2 December; Meredith recalled 10 December; his report of 986 kills with 9,860 rounds (ten per kill) plus
2,500 dead of wounds, disputed; his quoted comparison to an army division. Later: refused again in 1934, 1943
and 1948; the bounty, begun 1923, claims 57,034 bounties in six months of 1934. The verdict is left open:
"won" by the birds per a 1953 newspaper, "worked after all" per a 1935 one.

STORY ANGLE
Not "Australia lost to birds". A real policy fiasco with a human cause (veterans and a failed wheat
policy), told dry, and ending on what actually thinned the emus: a bounty. The hook's "The birds won" is the
popular verdict and the script answers it with "So who won?".

ONE-SENTENCE HOOK (verbatim into ideas.md and the script)
Australia sent soldiers with machine guns to defeat a bird. The birds won.

HISTORICAL PERIOD
1932, Great Depression; Western Australian wheatbelt. For the site: **Modern Era**.

LOCATION / CULTURAL CONTEXT
Campion (and the marginal farmland around Chandler and Walgoolan) in the Wheatbelt of Western Australia; Perth
and Canberra.

STORY TYPE
Historical event: Situation → Trigger → Escalation → Main event → Consequences.

KEY CHARACTERS
Major Gwynydd Purves Wynne-Aubrey Meredith (Royal Australian Artillery, 7th Heavy Battery); Sergeant S. McMurray
and Gunner J. O'Halloran; Sir George Pearce; the soldier-settlers; the Premier James Mitchell; the historian Greg
Beyer (quoted on the figures). The emus.

CENTRAL STORY QUESTION
Who actually won?

CORE CONFLICT
Armed veterans and soldiers against large, fast birds in open country, and then against the arithmetic of ammunition.

TURNING POINT
8 November: the withdrawal, and the return five days later.

PAYOFF
It took a bounty, not machine guns, to thin the emus.

KEY EVENTS (all from Wikipedia, *Emu War*, unless noted)
1. **E** Soldier-settlers; wheat prices "continued to fall" despite promised subsidies; "approximately 20,000 emus"
   (cited to a general ornithology textbook; local pages repeat it unattributed).
2. **E** Deputation to Pearce; his conditions: military personnel, WA government pays transport, farmers pay for
   food, accommodation and ammunition.
3. **E** 2 November: Campion, "some 50 emus"; out of range; settlers tried to herd them; "split into small groups".
4. **E** 4 November: ambush near a dam; "more than 1,000 emus"; "The gun jammed after only 12 birds were killed".
5. **S** A gun on a truck "proved to be ineffective, as the truck was unable to gain on the birds".
6. **E** By 8 November, 2,500 rounds fired; "one account estimates 50 birds, but other accounts range from 200 to
   500, the latter provided by the settlers"; "no casualties, except for their dignity".
7. **E** Withdrawal 8 November after negative press ("only a few" emus dead); Rowley James, a Lang Labor MP, asked
   Prime Minister Lyons mockingly whether a medal would be struck (West Australian, 9 Nov 1932; the exact date of
   the question is not given); Senator Dunn later called Pearce "Minister of the Emu War".
8. **E** Return 13 November (Premier Mitchell; a base commander's report of "300 emus killed"); "approximately 40
   emus killed" in two days; "approximately 100 emus per week" by 2 December; Meredith recalled 10 December.
9. **E** The report: "986 confirmed kills with 9,860 rounds, at a rate of exactly 10 rounds per confirmed kill";
   "exactly 2,500 wounded birds had also died"; Beyer: "highly disputed and is likely to be very inflated".
10. **E** The quotation: "If we had a military division with the bullet-carrying capacity of these birds, it
    would face any army in the world … They can face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks." Attributed to
    Meredith; the only cited source is *The Sunday Herald*, 5 July 1953, p. 13 — twenty years later. Three web
    pages give three slightly different wordings. The script says "is quoted as saying" and gives the first
    sentence only.
11. **E** Aftermath: requests refused in 1934, 1943 and 1948; "the bounty system that had been instigated in 1923
    was continued, and this proved to be effective: 57,034 bounties were claimed over a six-month period in 1934".
12. **S** "Emu War": the name came from the media; in 1953 the *Sunday Herald* wrote that the campaign "failed most
    miserably, and … brought for the bird its most complete victory"; the *Coolgardie Miner* of 23 August 1935 said
    the method "proved effective and saved what remained of the wheat".

CAUSES
Depression, a failed wheat policy, marginal land, drought, an emu movement into farmland after breeding.

CONSEQUENCES
No lasting effect on emu numbers ("minimal impact on the overall emu population", infobox); the bounty.

MOST INTERESTING FACTS
- Meredith's 986 is exactly a tenth of 9,860 rounds. (My arithmetic, not in the article: 9,860 is 140 fewer than the
  10,000 issued.)
- The soldiers' own words: "no casualties, except for their dignity".
- The emus split into small groups; "each pack seems to have its own leader" is from a 1953 piece — **not used**.

MYTHS / LEGENDS / TRADITIONS
None, but the story carries a legend: "the emus won". It is stated in the hook and tested at the end.

UNCERTAINTIES / DEBATES
1. Kills by 8 November: 50 vs "200 to 500" vs the base commander's 300 vs Meredith's 986 by 10 December, plus 2,500
   claimed dead of wounds. The script gives the range and then Meredith's claim with Beyer's doubt.
2. The 20,000: unattributed on every page; "by one count" in the script.
3. The quotation: attribution rests on a 1953 newspaper (Wikipedia) or "he wrote" in a letter (a popular page
   with no source). Hence "is quoted as saying".
4. Dates of the medal question and "Minister of the Emu War": the pages give only "November 1932".
5. Return interval: four days (Wikipedia, Pickering Brook) or five (a popular page); the script uses dates (8 and 13
   November), which agree.
6. Wikipedia infobox says "986 emus confirmed killed" as fact while the body calls it a claim.
7. Whether the emus "won": the sources disagree (1953 vs 1935).
8. An alternative origin story (Dan O'Leary as "instigator", Western Mail 1946) conflicts with the deputation
   account. Not used.
9. **Not checked at all:** Trove (blocked), Hansard, the National Archives file and Meredith's report, Murray
   Johnson's 2006 article (the main academic source; cross-host link not fetched).

WHY THIS VOICE FITS
The story is funnier told straight. A comic voice would turn serious farmers and soldiers into a joke.
