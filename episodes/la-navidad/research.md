# Research — The First Spanish Settlement in the Americas Was Built From a Shipwreck (stage 03)

TOPIC
New idea **#37** in `docs/ideas.md` (added in this session, out of the original 30): the wreck of the *Santa María* on
Christmas night 1492, the fort of La Navidad built from her timbers, the 39 men left in it, and what Columbus found
when he came back on 27 November 1493. The only story on the channel from the year 1492 itself (`el-dorado`, c. 1537, is the
other early-modern one); the user asked on 2026-11-16 for at least one from that period. Region: the Caribbean (north coast of Hispaniola, today Haiti). Popularity is **not
measured** (the search tool gives no view counts); Columbus's first landfall is retold everywhere, the wreck and
La Navidad rarely to the end.

**Premise corrected.** The first draft of the hook said "the first *European* settlement in the Americas". That is
wrong: the Norse built at L'Anse aux Meadows around 1000 CE. What is true, and what the sources say, is **the first
Spanish settlement in the Americas** (Euronews 2026) / the first European settlement "of the Age of Discovery"
(Wikipedia). The script says "Spanish".

RECOMMENDED STORY
Christmas Eve 1492, dead calm off northern Hispaniola. Columbus, awake two days and a night, goes below at about eleven;
the helmsman, against standing orders, leaves the tiller to a ship's boy; the current sets the *Santa María* on a
sandbank so gently that nobody feels it; the boy cries out. Columbus orders the master, Juan de la Cosa, to lay out an
anchor astern; the master and others take the boat towards the *Niña* to save themselves, and the *Niña* refuses them.
The ship falls on her side and her timbers open. The cacique Guacanagarí weeps, sends canoes, the ship is emptied in a
few hours and "not even a needle" is missing; he gives two large houses and a mask with gold at eyes and ears. Columbus
decides the loss was "a piece of good fortune": he would have sailed on and left nobody; many men ask leave to stay; he
orders a tower and fort built, names it Navidad, writes that the people are naked, unarmed and "hopelessly timid" and
that he could subjugate the island, and has a cannon fired through the hull of the wreck to show the cacique its force.
2 January: **39 men** left, with a year of bread and wine, trade goods, artillery and the ship's boat; he sails on 4
January. 27 November 1493: fleet of seventeen ships; two cannon fired at night, no answer; no one, no sign of houses
on the shore; the fort burned to the ground and some of the dead lying in the fields. Guacanagarí, in his hammock,
says: some died of disease, some went to the lands of the lord of the mines (Caonabó) and were killed there, the rest
were attacked in their houses. The place has never been proven: En Bas Saline, Guacanagarí's town, is "the most likely
candidate" (a burned structure on a mound standing in 1492, Spanish pottery, a lead musket ball, rat and pig bones);
a 2026 proposal says the coast has advanced and the wreck lies under ~6 m of sediment.

STORY ANGLE
A disaster that Columbus himself reads as luck, a settlement that exists only because of a sleeping crew, and a fort
that is destroyed in less than a year. The viewer is led by Columbus's own words (the journal) and by the one thing
the sources agree on: nobody has proved where it stood. Neither Columbus nor Guacanagarí is made a hero or a villain;
the Taíno side is given through what the Spaniards wrote down of it, and the script says once that this is all there is.

ONE-SENTENCE HOOK (verbatim into ideas.md and the script)
The first Spanish settlement in the Americas was built from a shipwreck. Within a year, nobody who lived in it was alive.

HISTORICAL PERIOD
24 December 1492 – 27 November 1493; excavations 1977–2003; for the site: **Modern Era** (c. 1492).

LOCATION / CULTURAL CONTEXT
North-west coast of Hispaniola near present Cap-Haïtien (Acul Bay, Haiti); the Taíno chiefdom of Marién under
Guacanagarí; the Spanish fleet of the first voyage.

STORY TYPE
Historical event / exploration: Known world → Motivation → Journey → Obstacle (the wreck) → Consequence → Mystery of
what remains.

KEY CHARACTERS
Christopher Columbus; Guacanagarí (cacique of Marién); Juan de la Cosa (master and owner of the *Santa María*); the
unnamed ship's boy and helmsman; Diego de Arana, Pedro Gutiérrez and Rodrigo de Escobedo (the three lieutenants left
in command); Dr. Diego Álvarez Chanca (physician of the second voyage, our witness for the return); Caonabó (lord of
the mines).

CENTRAL STORY QUESTION
What happened to the men Columbus left behind — and where did their town stand?

CORE CONFLICT
A fort built in a day to hold a foothold in a land the builders believed "timid", and a year of silence nobody on
either side recorded.

TURNING POINT
27 November 1493: the unanswered cannon shots.

PAYOFF
Columbus called the wreck good fortune; of the fort built from its timbers, nobody can yet point to one timber that
is beyond dispute.

KEY EVENTS (journal entries of Markham's translation, Hakluyt Society 1893, pp. 132–146; Chanca's letter; Florida Museum)
1. **E** 24–25 Dec: the helm, the boy, the sandbank "so gently that it could scarcely be felt"; the master flees in the
   boat; the *Niña* refuses him; masts cut away; the timbers open. Journal: "The Admiral had forbidden this throughout
   the voyage. The boys were never to be entrusted with the helm."
2. **E** Guacanagarí weeps (reported to Columbus by Arana and Gutiérrez); canoes unload the ship; "not even a needle
   missing"; two large houses; the mask with gold at eyes and ears; a feast (26 Dec).
3. **E** Columbus: "the disaster was really a piece of good fortune"; "many people had asked me to give them leave to
   remain"; the tower, fort and "large cellar"; "naked and without arms and hopelessly timid"; "I could subjugate the
   whole island". The name Villa de la Navidad "because the ship was lost on Christmas Day" (editor's note).
4. **E** 2 Jan: lombard fired into the side of the wreck "to show him the force of the lombards"; "39 men with the
   fortress" (the editor's note: the official list names **44**); the three lieutenants; supplies; carpenter, caulker,
   gunner, cooper, physician, tailor "all being seamen as well". 4 Jan: sails.
5. **E** 27 Nov 1493: Chanca: two lombards fired, no reply, nobody and no sign of houses on the shore. Guacanagarí
   "stretched upon his bed" (a hammock), his account (disease / killed at the mines of Caonabó / attacked in their
   houses). Chanca judges the dead "not yet two months" old.
6. **S** Wikipedia (*La Navidad*): "burned to the ground … nobody in the vicinity"; "bodies of eight Christians" in the
   fields; the men "began to quarrel among themselves, each taking as many women and as much gold as he could"; Caonabó
   set fire to the houses, men were forced into the sea.
7. **E** Archaeology: William Hodges (1977) found En Bas Saline; Florida Museum excavated 1983–1988 and returned in
   2003: "a very large classic Taíno town occupied between about AD 1200 and AD 1530", "the most likely candidate for La
   Navidad", a large burned structure on a mound with "nearly all of the European artifacts (including a lead musket
   ball and Spanish pottery) and European animal bones (rat and pig)", radiocarbon-dated as present in 1492; Puerto
   Real (1503) lies two km away. Euronews (12 Oct 2026): "the definitive proof is still missing"; historian María Luisa
   Cazorla argues the coast has advanced by sediment, so the wreck "would now lie on land, under some six metres of
   deposits", findable "within a radius of a few hundred metres" by geophysical prospecting.

12. **E** 23 Dec: Columbus's men visit Guacanagarí's town ("over 2,000 men", a "clean-swept" square, food from every
    inhabitant, the chief asks them to stay a day more). 26 Dec: the chief tells him of gold in Cibao and of the Caribs
    who raid his people; Columbus answers by signs that the sovereigns will have the Caribs destroyed.
13. **E** 29 Dec: Columbus writes that he had suspected the king "had worked against his knowing where the gold had its
    origin ... that he might not go away to barter in another part of the island". 30 Dec: five chiefs "all with their
    crowns"; Guacanagarí puts his crown on Columbus's head; Columbus gives a collar of coloured beads, a cloak, coloured
    boots and a silver ring. 31 Dec: with one ship left "it did not appear prudent" to go on discovering.
14. **E** 2 Jan: lombard fired through the wreck and a skirmish of armed crews "that the king might look upon the men who
    were left behind as friends, and that he might also have a proper fear of them"; "a statue of pure gold ... as big as
    the Admiral ... within ten days".

CAUSES
A tired admiral, a calm night, a forbidden handover of the tiller, a current; later, a settlement with no plan other
than gold.

CONSEQUENCES
Columbus founds La Isabela (east of Navidad) instead; the Spanish presence in the Caribbean starts with a lost fort.

MOST INTERESTING FACTS
- The master of the ship was also its owner (Juan de la Cosa, later a well-known pilot and mapmaker).
- Columbus fired a cannon *through the wreck* to show his host what it could do.
- The fort was built from the ship's own timbers; no timber of it has been identified beyond dispute.
- An Irishman from Galway ("Guillelmo, Irish") and an Englishman are on the list of the men left (editor's note).

MYTHS / LEGENDS / TRADITIONS
None. The famous "wept" detail is Columbus's own report, by two messengers. Treated as record, with the sources named.

UNCERTAINTIES / DEBATES
1. **How many men:** 39 (Columbus's journal); 36 (Wikipedia, citing another source); 44 (the official list of 1507,
   Navarrete). The script says "thirty-nine, by his own count".
2. **How many bodies / how they died:** eight in Wikipedia; "most of them disfigured" in Euronews; "all of the men dead"
   in the Florida Museum. The script says "some of the men lay dead" and that Guacanagarí gave three versions of deaths.
3. **Who destroyed it:** the account is Guacanagarí's (Chanca), passed through Columbus; historians "still wonder
   whether the leader who had welcomed them might have had a hand in it" (Euronews). Stated once.
4. **The "quarrelled over women and gold" account** is from Wikipedia and I could not trace it to a primary page; the
   script says "later Spanish accounts, built on what the Taíno told them".
5. **The site:** En Bas Saline "most likely"; not proven; the 2026 sediment hypothesis is one historian's proposal.
6. **Not used:** Barry Clifford's 2014 claim to have found the *Santa María* (disputed by the Haitian culture minister,
   per Euronews). The Nov 1493 arrival date (27 vs 28 Nov) differs by source; Chanca's editor gives the night of the 27th.

STORY STRUCTURE

RECOMMENDED VOICE
Dark / dramatic.

VOICE CHARACTER
Restrained, serious, unhurried, with weight on the quiet moments (the boy's cry, the unanswered cannon). Not
theatrical; the irony of Columbus's "good fortune" is delivered flat.

VOICE DELIVERY
~143 wpm. A short beat after "alive" in the hook. The boy's cry plain. "Not even a needle was missing" at normal pace,
without a smile. Columbus's words about the people slow and level; no commentary. The last two sentences quiet.

WHY THIS VOICE FITS
The story is a quiet disaster followed by a longer one. A warm voice would sweeten the wreck; a loud one would turn a
mystery into a lecture.
