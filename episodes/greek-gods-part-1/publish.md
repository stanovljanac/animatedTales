Three brothers shook three lots in a helmet, and whatever each of them drew, he would rule forever. This is how Zeus, Poseidon and Hades divided the universe of Greek mythology, and why the brother who drew worst became the god the Greeks mostly avoided naming.

It begins with their father. Cronus had been told that one of his children would overthrow him, so every time Rhea gave birth, he swallowed the child. When the sixth was coming, she hid in a cave on Crete and handed him a stone wrapped in a baby's blanket instead. That child was Zeus. He came back, forced his father to bring up his brothers and sisters, and what followed was a ten-year war against the Titans that neither side could finish.

The winners had a universe and no agreement about who owned it. According to Homer, they did not fight over it. They drew lots. But Poseidon never accepted the result, one part of the world was left out of the draw on purpose, and the brother who got the dead ended up with a very different name: Plouton, the rich one.
