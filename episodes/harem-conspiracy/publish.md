Three thousand years ago, an Egyptian court sentenced more than thirty people for a plot against their pharaoh. The record of their trial survives, and it never says what happened to the king.

This is the story of the harem conspiracy against Ramesses the Third. One of his wives, Tiye, wanted the throne for her son Pentawer, though it had been promised to another son. She found allies close to the king, from the chief of his chamber to a commander of the army, and the plotters even had wax figures made with a magic roll that belonged to Ramesses himself. What followed was a trial in which the accused lost even their names, and the court itself ended up on trial.

For more than a century, historians argued over whether the king had been murdered at all. Then, in 2012, researchers looked under the linen collar around his neck, while a young man in a coffin with no name, found in the same hidden tomb, raised a question that is still open.
