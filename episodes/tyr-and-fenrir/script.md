The gods wanted to bind a wolf.

But not because he was merely dangerous.

They feared what he would become.

His name was Fenrir.

Fenrir was one of Loki's monstrous children, and according to Norse tradition, the gods knew that he was destined to play a role in Ragnarök — the catastrophe that would bring the gods and their world to destruction.

So while Fenrir was still young, the gods decided that he could not be left unrestrained.

But there was a problem.

Fenrir kept growing.

And the stronger he became, the harder he was to control.

The gods first tried to bind him with a chain called Læding.

Fenrir pulled against it.

The chain broke.

They brought a stronger binding, called Drómi.

Again, Fenrir strained against it.

Again, he escaped.

The gods had learned something important.

They could not defeat Fenrir with strength alone.

So they sought something different.

They turned to the dwarfs, who created a magical binding called Gleipnir.

Unlike the heavy chains that had come before it, Gleipnir was remarkably light and thin.

Its strength came from things that, according to Norse tradition, could not normally be captured at all.

It was made from six impossible things: the sound of a cat's footsteps, the roots of mountains, the sinews of bears, the breath of fish, the spittle of birds, and the beard of a woman.

The gods brought Gleipnir to Fenrir.

But the wolf was suspicious.

He had already broken the bindings they had used against him.

This new one looked weaker than all of them.

Fenrir agreed to be bound only if the gods gave him a guarantee that they would release him.

And there was only one guarantee he would accept.

One of the gods had to place a hand inside his mouth.

The others hesitated.

Týr stepped forward.

He placed his hand between Fenrir's jaws.

Then the gods wrapped Gleipnir around the wolf.

Fenrir pulled.

He strained against the binding.

But this time, it did not break.

And Fenrir understood.

The gods had deceived him.

His jaws closed around Týr's hand.

The god lost it.

The gods had finally found something Fenrir could not break.

But they had paid for it with the hand of the god who had been willing to guarantee their promise.

And Fenrir was still alive.

He remained bound, waiting for the day when his bonds would finally break.

According to Norse tradition, that day would come at Ragnarök.

The gods had managed to restrain the wolf.

But they had never truly defeated him.
