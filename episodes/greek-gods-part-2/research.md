# Research — Greek Gods, Part 2: Hera, Demeter, Hestia (stage 03)

SERIES POSITION
Part 2 of 4. Spine, locked descriptions, the outro chain and the material boundaries against
Parts 1, 3 and 4 live in `docs/series/greek-gods.md`. This file researches **only Part 2**.
Part 1 covered the swallowing, the war and the draw; Part 2 re-tells that in one short paragraph
so the episode stands on its own, and points to Part 1 for the full story.

TOPIC
The three daughters of Cronus and what they were given when their brothers divided the universe.

RECOMMENDED STORY
Three brothers drew lots for the sky, the sea and the dead. Their three sisters — swallowed by the
same father, freed by the same brother — were not in the draw. Each got something smaller: a
marriage, a harvest, a hearth. Each story shows what that was worth. Hera is punished by the king
and still outranks him in stone; Demeter shuts down the harvest and forces both winning brothers to
bargain; Hestia takes the middle of every house and never leaves it.

STORY ANGLE
Part 1 ended on the brother who drew worst and turned out richest. Part 2 is the other half of the
family: the ones who drew nothing. The angle is that "nothing" was the part of the world people
actually live in — and that the one time a sister withheld her share, the king of the sky and the
king of the dead had to renegotiate.

ONE-SENTENCE HOOK
Three brothers drew lots for the universe; their three sisters were never given a lot.

HISTORICAL PERIOD
Myth, no date. Textual anchors: Hesiod, *Theogony* (c. 700 BCE); Homer, *Iliad* (c. 750–700 BCE);
*Homeric Hymn to Demeter* (c. 650–550 BCE); *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* (7th–6th c. BCE);
*Homeric Hymn* 29 *to Hestia*; Plato, *Phaedrus* (c. 370 BCE); Herodotus (5th c. BCE); Pausanias,
*Description of Greece* (2nd c. CE). Cult facts: Heraion at Olympia c. 600–580 BCE; Temple of Zeus
at Olympia c. 470–457 BCE; the Eleusinian Mysteries until the late 4th c. CE.

LOCATION / CULTURAL CONTEXT
Archaic and classical Greek religion. Named places: Olympus, Olympia, Arcadia (Stymphalos), Eleusis,
the underworld.

STORY TYPE
Myth / religion — World → Figure → Origin → Role → Conflict → Main story → Meaning, run three times
inside one frame.

KEY CHARACTERS
Hera, Demeter, Hestia. Necessary: Zeus, Hades, Persephone. Present in one line each: Cronus (recap),
Heracles, Hermes, the Sun (Helios), Poseidon and Apollo (Hestia's suitors).

CENTRAL STORY QUESTION
The brothers got the universe. What did the sisters get — and was it really less?

CORE CONFLICT
The sisters against a division that left them out, and above all against Zeus, who is the king,
the husband of one sister, the father of another sister's child, and the one who gives that child
away.

TURNING POINT
Demeter stops the grain. Until then the story shows what the sisters endured; from that moment it
shows what they held. The famine threatens not only mankind but the gods' own sacrifices, and the
king of the sky gives in.

PAYOFF
The brothers drew three places where no living person can stay. The sisters got the places where
people live — the marriage, the field, the hearth. Part 1 ended by saying every life arrives,
eventually, in Hades' kingdom. Part 2 answers it: every life ends in his kingdom, and is lived in
theirs.

**Recap (Part 1 material, one paragraph)**
1. Cronus, warned that a child will overthrow him, swallows each child at birth — in Hesiod's order
   Hestia, Demeter, Hera, Hades, Poseidon. [TRADITION — *Theogony* 453–467] The daughters come first,
   which the recap states.
2. Zeus is hidden, grows up, forces Cronus to bring the children back up; the war against the
   Titans; the draw of lots — sky, sea, the dead. [TRADITION — *Theogony* 468–500, 629–735; *Iliad*
   15.187–193] Told in full in Part 1; the narration points there.

**Hera — the marriage**
3. Hera is the last goddess Hesiod lists with Zeus ("Lastly, he made Hera his blooming wife"), after
   Metis, Themis, Eurynome, Demeter, Mnemosyne and Leto. [TRADITION — *Theogony* 886–923] Cut from the
   script for time (and because it names Metis and Leto, both Part 3).
4. Queen of the gods and goddess of marriage. [TRADITION / RECORD of cult — Hera Teleia]
5. Hera cannot act against Zeus directly and pursues his lovers and their children. [TRADITION —
   the pattern across the myths of Heracles, Semele, Io, Leto; the script states the pattern and
   names only Heracles.]
6. When Heracles sails from Troy (the earlier sack of Troy, not the Trojan War), Hera has Sleep put
   Zeus to sleep and sends storm winds that drive Heracles to Kos. [TRADITION — *Iliad* 14.249–261]
7. Zeus wakes, and hangs Hera in mid-air: two anvils fastened to her feet, her hands bound in an
   unbreakable golden chain, among the clouds; the other gods cannot reach her, and any he catches
   he hurls from the threshold of Olympus. [TRADITION — *Iliad* 15.18–30] The punishment is for the
   storm against Heracles — **not** for the plot to bind Zeus (*Iliad* 1.396–406), which later
   retellings often attach to it.
8. The Heraion at Olympia (c. 600–580 BCE), one of the earliest Doric temples in Greece, stood more
   than a century before the Temple of Zeus there (completed c. 457 BCE). [RECORD — archaeology]
9. Herodotus calls Hera's temple on Samos the greatest temple he knew. [RECORD — Herodotus 3.60]
   Cut from the script for time.
10. At old Stymphalos in Arcadia, Temenus, who raised Hera, founded three sanctuaries and gave her
    three names: Girl (Pais) while a maiden, Grown-up/Wife (Teleia) when married to Zeus, and Widow
    (Chera) "when for some cause or other she quarrelled with Zeus and came back to Stymphalus".
    [TRADITION of cult, reported by Pausanias 8.22.2]

**Demeter — the harvest**
11. Demeter bears Persephone to Zeus. [TRADITION — *Theogony* 912–914]
12. Persephone is gathering flowers — roses, crocuses, violets, irises, hyacinths and a narcissus
    Earth grew "at the will of Zeus" as a snare — when the earth opens and Hades rises in a golden
    chariot and carries her off. Zeus had given her to him. [TRADITION — *Hymn to Demeter* 1–20]
13. For nine days Demeter wanders the earth with flaming torches in her hands, tasting neither
    ambrosia nor nectar and not bathing. On the tenth dawn Hecate meets her and they go to Helios,
    who sees everything. [TRADITION — *Hymn* 40–63] The script compresses Hecate out.
14. Helios: Zeus gave her to Hades; Hades is "no unfitting husband … your own brother", and has
    "that third share which he received when division was made at the first". [TRADITION — *Hymn*
    75–87] **The Hymn itself refers back to the draw** — the script's bridge to Part 1 is ancient,
    not invented.
15. Demeter leaves the gods, comes to Eleusis disguised as an old woman, nurses the prince
    Demophon, is discovered, reveals herself and has a temple built. [TRADITION — *Hymn* 90–300]
    Cut from the script to one clause ("her temple at Eleusis").
16. Sitting in the temple, she causes "a most dreadful and cruel year": the seed does not sprout,
    oxen draw ploughs in vain, barley is sown without avail; she would have destroyed mankind and
    robbed the Olympians of their sacrifices. [TRADITION — *Hymn* 305–313]
17. Zeus sends Iris, then all the gods one after another, offering gifts and honours; she refuses,
    vowing not to return to Olympus or let the earth bear until she sees her daughter. [TRADITION —
    *Hymn* 314–333]
18. Zeus sends Hermes to Erebus; Hades obeys, but secretly gives Persephone a pomegranate seed to
    eat. [TRADITION — *Hymn* 334–374]
19. Terms: having eaten, she must spend a third part of the seasons below, the other two with her
    mother and the gods. [TRADITION — *Hymn* 398–403, 445–447] The **third** is the Hymn's; "half the
    year" is later (Ovid). The script uses "a third".
20. Demeter lets the fields bear again and teaches the rulers of Eleusis her Mysteries: "Happy is he
    … who has seen these mysteries; but he who is uninitiate … never has lot of like good things once
    he is dead." [TRADITION — *Hymn* 470–482] The Mysteries were celebrated at Eleusis until the
    sanctuary's end in the late 4th c. CE. [RECORD] Cut from the script for time; see OPTIONAL.

**Hestia — the hearth**
21. First-born of Cronus; the *Hymn to Aphrodite* calls her "first-born … and youngest too" —
    the standard explanation (Evelyn-White's note; Kerényi) is first swallowed, last brought up —
    the stone, swallowed last, came up first (*Theogony* 497). [TRADITION + INTERPRETATION of the
    paradox] The script states the order of swallowing and return, not the epithet.
22. Poseidon and Apollo both seek to marry her; she refuses and, touching the head of Zeus, swears a
    great oath to remain a maiden all her days. Zeus gives her "a high honour instead of marriage":
    her place in the midst of the house and the richest portion; a share of honour in all temples;
    chief of goddesses among mortals. [TRADITION — *Hymn to Aphrodite* 21–32]
23. Without Hestia mortals hold no banquet: sweet wine is poured to her "both first and last".
    [TRADITION / RECORD of practice — *Homeric Hymn* 29]
24. Newborns were carried around the household hearth at the Amphidromia. [RECORD of practice —
    Plato *Theaetetus* 160e; Suda] Cut from the script for time.
25. Greek cities kept a public hearth in the prytaneion; colonists took fire from the mother city's
    hearth to the new foundation. [RECORD of practice — Herodotus 1.146 on colonists setting out
    from the Athenian prytaneion; later lexica and scholia for the fire]
26. In Plato's procession of the gods, Zeus drives a winged chariot and the gods follow in eleven
    companies; "Hestia alone remains in the house of the gods". [TRADITION as philosophy — *Phaedrus*
    247a] The script does **not** say Plato explains her lack of myths; it places the two facts side
    by side.

MOST INTERESTING FACTS
- **The Hymn to Demeter quotes the lottery.** Helios justifies the abduction by Hades' "third share
  … received when division was made". Part 2's link to Part 1 is in the source.
- **Hera the Widow**, with an immortal husband — a cult title, not a modern joke.
- **Hera's temple at Olympia is older than Zeus's**, in Zeus's own sanctuary.
- **The hanging with anvils** — the queen of the gods suspended between sky and earth.
- **Demeter's famine threatens the gods, not only people**: no harvest, no sacrifices.
- **Eldest and youngest at once** — Hestia's paradox comes straight out of the swallowing.
- **First and last libation** to Hestia at every feast.
- **Colonists carried the fire** from the old city to the new.
- **Hestia alone stays home** while the gods ride out.

MYTHS / LEGENDS / TRADITIONS
The whole episode is tradition. The narration names the *Homeric Hymn to Demeter* implicitly (the
story is told as its story), Hesiod through the recap, and Plato and cult practice where it steps
outside the myth.

UNCERTAINTIES / DEBATES
- **Hestia "gave up her throne to Dionysus" is modern.** It comes from Robert Graves, *The Greek
  Myths* (1955); no ancient source tells it. What is ancient is only that lists of the twelve gods
  vary: the Altar of the Twelve Gods in the Athenian Agora is taken to have included Hestia, while
  the Parthenon east frieze is usually read as having Dionysus. The series bible (`§7`) listed
  "Hestija ustupa presto" as Part 2 material; it is **not used**, and the bible is corrected.
- **Which season Persephone is below** — the Hymn gives a third of the year and ties her return to
  spring; whether the absence is winter or the Greek summer drought is debated. The script says
  "a third of every year" and names no season.
- **Heraion at Olympia**: Pausanias (5.17.1) saw images of both Hera and Zeus in it, and some
  scholars think the temple was shared at first. The script says "her temple", which is its name.
- **Hera's pursuit of Zeus's lovers** is a pattern across many sources, not one text.

AVOIDED ON PURPOSE
- **Leto and the birth of Apollo and Artemis** — Part 3's spine; Hera's persecution of Leto is not
  mentioned. Apollo appears only as a suitor of Hestia.
- **Metis and Athena's birth** — Part 3; teased in the outro as "one of them straight out of her
  father's head", never explained.
- **Hera throws Hephaestus from Olympus** — Part 4.
- **Io** — idea #29, a possible episode of its own.
- **Hera, Poseidon and Athena plot to bind Zeus** (*Iliad* 1.396–406) — strong, but it pulls Athena
  in and is often confused with the anvil punishment.
- **The cuckoo courtship** (Pausanias 2.17.4) and **Hera's yearly bath at Kanathos** (Pausanias
  2.38.2) — good, cut for time.
- **Demophon in the fire** — the Eleusis episode is compressed to "her temple at Eleusis".

STORY STRUCTURE
Beginning — the lots, and the sisters who never got one.
Development — the recap: the same father, the same brother, the draw; what the sisters got instead.
Escalation — Hera: queen, betrayed, punished, and still honoured above the king in stone.
Turning point — Demeter: the stolen daughter, the "third share", the year without harvest, and the
king of the sky giving in.
Payoff — Hestia, who wanted nothing and was given the middle of every house; then the frame closes:
lives end in Hades' kingdom, and are lived in theirs.

VOICE CHARACTER

VOICE DELIVERY
~160 wpm — the pace Part 1 was actually delivered at. The recap brisk and even; slow down from
"The sisters got no kingdom." A full beat before each sister's one-line opener. Hera cool and dry;
Demeter builds through the famine; Hestia quieter than everything before her. The last two
sentences slow and unpushed.

WHY THIS VOICE FITS
Series continuity is not optional: Part 2 is the same story told from the other side of the table,
and the viewer should hear the same narrator. The material also wants the register — a stolen
daughter, a year of famine, a queen hung from the sky — told with restraint rather than drama.
