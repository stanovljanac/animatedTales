# Research — The Antikythera Mechanism (stage 03)

TOPIC
The Antikythera mechanism (idea #16, `docs/ideas.md`).

RECOMMENDED STORY
Sponge divers stumble on a 2,000-year-old wreck; among its treasures is an ignored lump of
bronze that turns out to be a hand-cranked geared model of the sky.

STORY ANGLE
"The machine that shouldn't exist" — then the honest turn: it isn't impossible, ancient writers
described such devices. What is unsettling is that exactly one survived, and only because it sank.

ONE-SENTENCE HOOK (verbatim from ideas.md)
Divers pulled this machine from a shipwreck. Then scientists realized what it could calculate.

HISTORICAL PERIOD
Built probably 2nd century BCE (debated: c. 205 – c. 87 BCE); wreck c. 70–60 BCE;
discovery 1900–1902; modern analysis 1950s–2005+.

LOCATION / CULTURAL CONTEXT
Hellenistic Greek world; wreck off Antikythera (between Kythera and Crete); finds taken to the
National Archaeological Museum, Athens.

STORY TYPE
Mystery / technology (Question → Evidence → Explanation → What remains unknown).

KEY CHARACTERS
Unnamed Symi sponge diver (Elias Stadiatis in most accounts); captain Dimitrios Kontos;
archaeologist Valerios Stais; (unnamed in script) Derek de Solla Price, 2005 research team.

CENTRAL STORY QUESTION
What was this thing, and how could it exist so early?

CORE CONFLICT
The object versus everyone's assumption of what ancient people could build.

TURNING POINT
The scans reveal the gear trains and inscriptions; the purpose becomes readable.

PAYOFF
It is not out of place — Cicero described such devices (one attributed to Archimedes). The loss
is the mystery: bronze was melted down; the sea is the only reason one survived.

KEY EVENTS
1. Spring 1900: Symi sponge divers shelter from a storm off Antikythera. [RECORD]
2. Diver surfaces claiming a heap of corpses and horses on the seabed. [TRADITION — crew anecdote]
3. Captain Kontos dives: bronze and marble statues around an ancient wreck. [RECORD]
4. 1900–1901 salvage with Hellenic Royal Navy support, ~45 m+; one diver dies, two paralysed. [RECORD]
5. Mechanism raised 1901 among statues, glass, pottery, unnoticed. [RECORD]
6. 17 May 1902: Valerios Stais notices a gear wheel in a fragment. [RECORD]
7. Doubts it is ancient; some suggest a later intrusion. [RECORD / DEBATE]
8. 1971: Price & Karakalos X-ray/gamma radiography; *Gears from the Greeks*, 1974. [RECORD]
9. 2005: Antikythera Mechanism Research Project, X-ray CT + surface imaging; ~30 gears,
   thousands of inscription characters. [RECORD]

MOST INTERESTING FACTS
- Case c. 34 × 18 × 9 cm; 82 fragments; at least 30 surviving bronze gears.
- Front: Sun and Moon through zodiac, 365-day Egyptian calendar ring, rotating moon-phase ball.
- Back: Metonic spiral (19 years / 235 months), Saros eclipse spiral (223 months), games dial
  (four-year cycle incl. Olympia).
- Pin-and-slot on offset axles models the Moon's varying speed (lunar anomaly, Hipparchan theory).
- Planet display inferred from inscriptions; hardware lost. [INTERPRETATION]
- Comparable complexity next known in 14th-c. European astronomical clocks (Richard of
  Wallingford, Giovanni de' Dondi).
- Cicero (*De re publica*) describes Archimedes' sphere brought to Rome by Marcellus;
  (*De natura deorum*) mentions one by Posidonius. [RECORD of texts]

UNCERTAINTIES / DEBATES
Date of construction; maker/workshop (Rhodes? Corinthian calendar); exact destination of the
ship; planetary gearing reconstructions are models, not surviving parts.

AVOIDED ON PURPOSE
The script says "about thirty surviving gears" and does not state which gear has 223 teeth —
sources disagree between the large drive wheel and the Saros train gear.

ESSENTIAL MATERIAL
Discovery anecdote, statues reveal, human cost of salvage, ignored lump, gear in 1902, doubt,
2005 scans, sky functions (front, back, moon anomaly), Cicero/Archimedes, bronze recycled, sea
preserved it.

OPTIONAL MATERIAL (cut)
Price by name, Metonic numbers, Corinthian month names, Hipparchus, Byzantine geared sundial,
2021 planetary reconstruction, Jacques Cousteau 1976 dives, 2016 skeleton find.

RECOMMENDED VOICE
Explorer / discovery.

VOICE CHARACTER
Warm, curious, mature narrator who has just found something and wants to show you. Clear,
unhurried, never theatrical.

VOICE DELIVERY
~140 wpm. Quieter and slightly ominous through the dive ("bodies", "paralyzed"); brighter and
wondering from "You turned a handle on the side." Short pauses after one-line paragraphs
("A gear wheel." / "Not quite."). Land the final paragraph slowly without a push.
