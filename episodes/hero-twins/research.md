# Research — Two Brothers Beat the Maya Lords of Death at Their Own Game (stage 03)

TOPIC
New idea #36 in `docs/ideas.md` (added 2026-11-04, outside the original thirty): the Hero Twins of the
Popol Vuh, the K'iche' Maya book of Guatemala — the ball game against the lords of Xibalba, the
underworld. First Maya / Mesoamerican story on the channel and the one that fills the **North America**
region (Mesoamerica is North America on the site's world map; `three-underworlds` has a Cherokee tale
but the site records one region per episode). The user had asked for Maya and Inca stories; the first South
American story is `el-dorado` (Muisca), so this one covers the Maya. Popularity is **not measured**.

RECOMMENDED STORY
Told in the order of the book: (1) the father Hun Hunahpu and his brother, whose ball game above Xibalba
brings an invitation from the lords by owl messengers; they greet two wooden figures as if they were
lords, sit on a bench that is a burning stone, are locked in the House of Darkness with a torch and a
cigar each to be returned unburned, fail, are sacrificed and buried in the ballcourt; the father's head
in a tree becomes fruit. (2) A young woman touches the fruit; the head spits into her hand; she
conceives twins, Hunahpu and Xbalanque. (3) The twins play ball and the lords send the same invitation; it
reaches them by a louse inside a toad inside a snake inside a hawk; they plant two canes in the middle of
their grandmother's house as signs of life. (4) They cross the rivers on their blowguns; at the
crossroads a mosquito bites each lord in turn and each lord's cry gets him named, so the twins greet
every lord by name and refuse the burning bench. (5) Houses: Darkness (macaw feathers and fireflies for
torch and cigar), Cold, Jaguars (thrown bones), Bats (Hunahpu looks out for the dawn and loses his
head). Xbalanque calls the animals; a turtle takes the head's place; in the ball game a rabbit lures
the lords away and he swaps the heads back. (6) The twins arrange their own death by telling two
soothsayers what to answer; they throw themselves on the oven; their bones are ground and put in the
river but sink and become youths, then fish-men, then ragged dancers who burn and restore a house,
sacrifice each other and rise. (7) The lords ask to be treated the same; the twins kill the two chief
lords, One Death and Seven Death, and do not revive them. (8) They honour their father and rise as sun
and moon. Closing: the twins won because they had planned their own death, and the lords could not resist
a show.

STORY ANGLE
Not "Maya mythology in general". The reversal: the game that killed the father is won by the sons by
dying and returning on their own terms; the lords lose because they want to see the trick done to
them. The script states once, near the end, that nobody knows how old the story is and that the book
was written down after the conquest.

ONE-SENTENCE HOOK (verbatim into ideas.md and the script)
The lords of the Maya underworld invited two brothers to a ball game. It was a trap, and the brothers
knew it. It was how their father had died.

HISTORICAL PERIOD
Myth, no fixed date. The book was recorded in K'iche' around 1550–1558 (Recinos and Tedlock suggest 1554–
1558) and copied by the Dominican Francisco Ximénez around 1701 at Chichicastenango; Classic Maya art
(200–900 AD) has been read as showing the Twins. For the site: **Myth & Legend**.

LOCATION / CULTURAL CONTEXT
The K'iche' highlands of Guatemala; Xibalba, "place of fright", the Maya underworld; the Mesoamerican
ballgame (*pitz*), played in a court, linked in the text to death and its overcoming.

STORY TYPE
Myth / religion: World → Figure → Origin → Role → Conflict → Main story → Meaning.

KEY CHARACTERS
Hun Hunahpu and Vucub Hunahpu (father and uncle); Hunahpu and Xbalanque (twins); their grandmother; the
lords of Xibalba — One Death (Hun-Came) and Seven Death (Vucub-Came) and ten more lords in pairs
(Flying Scab, Gathered Blood, Pus Demon, Jaundice Demon, Bone Staff, Skull Staff, Sweepings Demon,
Stabbing Demon, Wing, Packstrap — Wikipedia, *Xibalba*).

CENTRAL STORY QUESTION
How do you beat the lords of death at their own game, when the game killed your father?

CORE CONFLICT
Two brothers against lords who cheat and kill, and a father to avenge.

TURNING POINT
"But the twins knew their end": the twins plan their own death.

PAYOFF
The lords of death lose because they cannot resist a good show.

KEY EVENTS
All from the Spanish Wikisource text (see SOURCES). Names in that translation are rendered in meaning:
Supremo Maestro Mago = Hun Hunahpu, Principal Maestro Mago = Vucub Hunahpu, Maestro Mago = Hunahpu,
Brujito = Xbalanque, Supremo Muerto / Principal Muerto = One Death / Seven Death, Maestro Mono and Maestro
Simio = Hun Batz and Hun Chouen (not in the script), Antigua Ocultadora = the grandmother.
1. **E** (ch. 11) The father and uncle play ball near Xibalba; the lords: "Who are they who begin to play
   over our heads?"; invitation by four owl messengers. (ch. 12) They leave their ball in a hole in the
   roof, say goodbye, cross the barrancos and rivers, reach the crossroads of four roads, meet the lords:
   first two wooden figures, greeted as lords; "a bench of burning stone" — they sit, burn; the Dark
   House with a pine torch and a cigar each, "to be returned intact"; both consumed; sacrificed and buried
   in the ballcourt "of the Sacrifices"; the father's head in a tree that bore fruit "like the calabash".
2. **E** (ch. 13) A lord's daughter touches the fruit; "the bone … spat saliva into the palm"; "In that
   saliva I have given you my posterity". She conceives; the twins are born (ch. 15).
3. **E** (ch. 18) The twins sweep their father's ballcourt; "who are those who begin now to play over our
   heads?"; "Que vengan … dentro de siete días jugaremos". The grandmother's louse is swallowed by a toad,
   the toad by a snake, the snake by a hawk; the hawk is shot, the louse delivers the message; they plant two
   canes "in the middle of the house": "If they dry, a sign of our death … if they bud, you will say: they
   live."
4. **E** (ch. 19) They pass the rivers "on their blowguns"; at the crossroads they send a mosquito to bite
   each lord in order; each cries "¡Ay!" and the next names him; they greet each lord by name and refuse the
   burning bench.
5. **E** (ch. 19–22) Darkness (a macaw's tail feather for the torch and fireflies for the cigar); the
   second trial in this translation is the House of Obsidian (arrows; ants steal flowers from the
   lords' garden — not used); Cold; Jaguars ("arrojaron huesos"); Fire; Bats: they sleep in their
   blowguns; Hunahpu looks out at the dawn and the Death Bat cuts off his head; Xbalanque calls the animals
   and the turtle becomes the head; the lords' ball game, a rabbit set loose, the heads swapped; the
   turtle "broke like a clay pot".
6. **E** (ch. 22–23) They tell two soothsayers what to answer; the lords make a hot stone like a grill/oven;
   "No os burléis así de nosotros. ¿No sabemos que moriremos, oh jefes?"; they embrace and throw
   themselves on the oven; the lords rejoice; bones ground and scattered in the river; the bones sink and
   become youths; on the fifth day fish-men; the next day two ragged wanderers; dances (Owl, Weasel,
   Armadillo, Centipede, Stilts); a burnt-and-restored house; a dog sacrificed and revived (not used); a man's
   heart taken and he revives (not used); they sacrifice each other.
7. **E** The lords: "Haced [lo mismo] con nosotros, sacrificadnos"; they sacrifice One Death first, then
   seize Seven Death "without reviving his face"; the others flee, then surrender; the twins give their
   names and punish: no more power; "only griddles, pots"; the lords' ball game "the offspring of the weeds".
8. **E** (ch. 24–25) The canes sprout twice and the grandmother burns copal to them; the twins honour
   their father at the ballcourt; "Se elevaron … subieron de repente a los cielos. Y el uno fue el sol, el
   otro la luna"; the 400 youths killed by Zipacna become stars.

CAUSES
Noise above Xibalba; the lords' vanity; a father to avenge.

CONSEQUENCES
In the text, the lords lose their power over men; the twins rise as sun and moon.

MOST INTERESTING FACTS
- The mosquito scouting: the lords name each other in their pain.
- The canes planted in the middle of the house as a sign of life.
- The father's head spits into a young woman's hand.
- The twins tell the soothsayers what to say so the lords will do the very thing that lets them return.

MYTHS / LEGENDS / TRADITIONS
All TR. The script tells the story as the book tells it and flags the age and transmission question once.

UNCERTAINTIES / DEBATES
1. **Age and transmission.** The Popol Vuh was "originally preserved through oral tradition until approximately
   1550"; the writer is unknown; the surviving text is Ximénez's c. 1701 transcription with Spanish
   translation, and a minority "disputes the existence of pre-Ximénez texts". Wikipedia's lead says the story is
   "from long before the Spanish conquest"; its body says the origin question is contested. The script: "No one
   knows how old the story is … written down after the conquest".
2. **The Twins in Classic art.** "Identified in the art of the Classic Mayas (200–900 AD)"; San Bartolo
   (Preclassic) murals may show Hunahpu; the Princeton vase is **not mentioned in any page read**. The script
   says "may be the twins".
3. **Versions differ**, so the script uses only what the Spanish text and Wikipedia agree on:
   - The houses: Raynaud/Asturias — Darkness, Obsidian, Cold, Jaguars, Fire, Bats; Spence — Gloom, Lances,
     Cold, Tigers, Fire, Bats; Wikipedia — Dark, Razor, Cold, Jaguar, Fire (Hot House), Bat.
   - The father's body after the victory: retrieved (Wikipedia, *Maya Hero Twins*) or left buried in the
     ballcourt (*Mesoamerican ballgame*); the Spanish text has funerals at the ballcourt and the father "not
     naming" the Magicians. Not used beyond "honored their father".
   - Spence's retelling has the lords asking to be slain, not tricked — it agrees with the Spanish text.
   - Who rises as sun and moon: the twins in the Spanish text and Wikipedia; Spence's summary says the father and
     uncle. The script follows the Spanish text.
4. **Human sacrifice and the ballgame.** Wikipedia: the association "appears rather late in the archaeological
   record"; its depictions are hedged ("it is assumed that these captives were sacrificed"). The script does
   not claim that ball games routinely ended in sacrifice.
5. **The grandmother's name and the wife Xquic**: the script names none; they vary by spelling.

RECOMMENDED VOICE
Ancient / mysterious.

VOICE CHARACTER
Deep, deliberate, slightly dark, with a small smile for the clever parts.

VOICE DELIVERY
~143 wpm. Slow for the father's death; brisk and playful for the animal messengers and the tricks; level
and quiet for the oven; lighter for the dance; "Do the same to us" plain; calm ending.

WHY THIS VOICE FITS
The lords of death are vain, not furious; a dramatic voice would make them monsters, a documentary voice
would flatten the tricks. A firelit voice lets both the dread and the humour stand.
